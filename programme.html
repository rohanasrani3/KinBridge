Parenting Foundations

Four lessons. Skills for your child — and support for you.

Parenting skills built alongside confidence, self-efficacy and stress management — because how you feel shapes how your child does.

Lesson 1

Understanding Your Child’s Changing Needs

Key aspects of child and adolescent development, and how they shape emotions and behaviour.

Daily practice · 1 min: before you reply in a tense moment, take one breath and guess what your child is feeling.
Lesson 2

Encouraging Positive Behaviour

Positive reinforcement, realistic expectations, and helping children build confidence and self-efficacy.

FocusConfidence & self-efficacy

Lesson 3

Managing Stress and Strengthening Family Bonds

Recognise your own stress, practise simple coping strategies, and build a supportive, secure parent–child relationship.

FocusParent stress management

Lesson 4

Communicating with Empathy

Active listening, constructive responses, and handling disagreements without escalating conflict.

Daily practice · 3 min: listen for three minutes without giving advice, then say back what you heard.

Every session

Practice, not lectures.

Knowing isn't the same as doing. Each 90-minute session follows the same three steps.

1
Scenario

Work through a real Hong Kong family moment together — what happened, and what could work.

2
Reflect

A short prompt on your own reactions and what you'd do differently.

3
Daily micro-practice

Leave with a 1–3 minute action to try each day. Check in with one tap in the app, then take 5 minutes to reflect at the end of the week.

Safety first

Education, not therapy.

KinBridge teaches everyday parenting skills in groups. It is not individual clinical therapy, and it is not a crisis service.

Who leads the sessions

Every group is led by a Registered Social Worker (RSW), an Educational Psychologist, or another health professional with experience in parenting education.

In an emergency, call 999. For urgent support, call the 24-hour Mental Health Support Hotline 18111.

Screening and referral

  • Every parent completes a short wellbeing screen before joining.
  • It looks for signs that need specialist care — such as severe depression or conduct problems, possible ADHD or autism, or a parent in mental-health crisis.
  • If a flag comes up, we help you connect with the right NGO or clinical service before, or alongside, the course.
  • Facilitators follow the same referral steps if concerns arise during the course.
